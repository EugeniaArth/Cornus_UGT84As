# Directly characterized β-glucogallin-forming UGTs

## Scope and curation rule

This report covers published plant UGTs for which the paper reports recombinant-enzyme or equivalent functional evidence that gallic acid plus UDP-glucose produces 1-O-galloyl-β-D-glucose (β-glucogallin, βG). The core table is limited to proteins explicitly designated as UGT84A/UGT84B in the literature, or subsequently mapped to that subfamily. A positive βG enzyme assay is not, by itself, proof that a protein is UGT84A; proteins with older generic GT names are kept in a separate table until their subfamily assignment is verified.

Accession numbers below were checked against the primary article text/tables where available and NCBI Nucleotide/Protein records. The protein accession is taken from the GenBank CDS `protein_id` qualifier for the linked nucleotide record. For recent studies with no individual sequence accession in the article, the absence is stated rather than inferred from a project-level accession. The literature was cross-checked across primary reports, later studies/reviews, and sequence records; a later citation is not treated as an independent replication of an enzyme assay.

## Important comparator and interpretation points

- The 2018 *Eucalyptus* study reported UGT84J3, UGT84J4, and UGT84J5 as inactive in its gallic-acid/UDP-glucose assays, while the four UGT84A proteins were active. Their nucleotide records are LC189072.1, LC189073.1, and LC189074.1, respectively. These are useful experimentally tested negative comparators, not positive βG references. [5]
- Conversely, *L. cubeba* UGT84B1 is reported to form βG. Therefore, do not encode the expectation that every UGT84B is inactive; include UGT84B1 explicitly as a known positive comparator when testing Cornus placement.
- *Juglans regia* JrGGT1/UGT84A73 and JrGGT2/UGT84A74 are relevant tannin-pathway candidates, but the 2021 study describes their βG-forming role as predicted/inferred rather than demonstrating the same purified-enzyme βG reaction. They are not in the direct-positive table.
- The 2019 pomegranate UGT72BD1 produces gallic-acid 4-O-glucoside (an ether-linked product), not βG; it is excluded from this set. [11]
- Direct in-vitro product formation and in-planta pathway contribution are separate evidence types. The table does not imply that every enzyme has equally strong in-vivo evidence or identical substrate specificity.

## What this implies for the proposed UGT84 phylogeny

The hypothesis that Cornus UGT84A proteins will be closer to UGT84A βG-formers than to UGT84B/J is testable. However, the stronger claim that βG activity is absent outside UGT84A should be dropped: the directly reported LcUGT84B1 result already contradicts it. A better test is whether Cornus candidates group with established UGT84A proteins, while retaining UGT84B1 as an experimentally positive subfamily comparator and UGT84J3–J5 as experimentally tested negative comparators.

## PF00201 HMM: recommended use in this project

The user's Pfam 29.0 PF00201 profile is suitable as a **broad UDP-glycosyltransferase-domain screen**, not as a UGT84 classifier or a functional predictor.

1. Preserve the downloaded profile and its version for reproducibility. Before using HMMER's `--cut_tc`, inspect the HMM header and confirm that trusted-cutoff (`TC`) values are present; `--cut_tc` only has defined behavior when the profile supplies them. Record the profile checksum and HMMER version with the results.
2. On the annotated *C. florida* proteome, use PF00201 to retrieve broad UGT candidates. InterPro/domain validation and PSPG-motif inspection are useful checks, but the domain and PSPG motif occur across many family-1 UGTs and do not identify UGT84A or βG activity.
3. Classify subfamily membership by curated sequence comparison and phylogeny against the directly characterized panel above, including both positive UGT84B1 and negative UGT84J controls. Do not call candidates functional based on an HMM hit.
4. A custom UGT84 profile may be built from verified UGT84A/B/J sequences as a **secondary sensitivity search**. It should not replace the broad PF00201 pass, and a profile built only from *C. florida* hits risks circularity and loss of divergent copies.
5. For unannotated genomes, do not expect a protein-domain HMM search alone to resolve genes from raw genomic DNA or exon structure. Combine protein-to-genome mapping, nucleotide-based locus finding, splice-aware gene-model refinement, and manual curation as already planned.

The paper workflow that builds a species-specific UGT HMM can be useful for defining a broad species-specific UGT repertoire, but it is not the best sole approach for this subfamily question: a model trained on a narrow or incomplete seed can inherit its omissions and bias. Use PF00201 for broad recovery, then use curated phylogenetic and homology evidence for UGT84 assignment.
